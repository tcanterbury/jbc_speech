Credits & thanks

This little library stands on the generous work of many people. We're deeply grateful to every one of them for sharing what they made. Without them, it simply wouldn't exist.

The recordings: Prof. John Esling and Jill House

Thank you, wholeheartedly, to Prof. John Esling and Jill House for recording the sounds of the IPA so carefully and for allowing them to be shared. Hearing a symbol spoken by an expert makes all the difference, and their recordings are the heart of this add-in's listening feature.

The recordings come from the International Phonetic Association's interactive IPA chart, retrieved November 26, 2026. Copyright remains with the respective rights holders. With thanks as well to the resources the chart credits: John Esling's University of Victoria IPA charts, and, for Jill House's recordings, Sounds of the IPA and its vowel chart resources.

The original MP3 files are included unchanged, for personal, noncommercial use. Nothing has been trimmed, converted, or otherwise processed. As the chart's credits and licensing page sets out, they're shared under Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial–NoDerivatives 4.0. That means: credit the creators, keep the license information, and don't redistribute adapted recordings or use them commercially under this license. We're glad to honour those terms.

Not every symbol has a recording from each speaker. Some clips contain example syllables, words, or contrasting sounds; where the chart supplies several examples, they play in sequence.

The IPA chart: the International Phonetic Association

Our sincere thanks to the International Phonetic Association for the International Phonetic Alphabet itself, and for making the IPA chart (revised to 2020) freely available under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 license, © 2020 International Phonetic Association. The Chart view follows the chart's arrangement and labels, and the List view uses its standard categories. Neither reproduces the chart artwork.

The software that makes it run

  • Microsoft, for Office.js and the Office Add-ins platform, and for the open-source office-addin-debugging, office-addin-dev-certs and office-addin-manifest tools (MIT license) used to build and test it. Thank you for making Word so extensible.
  • The Node.js project and the OpenJS Foundation, for Node.js (MIT license). It runs the local server, and the Windows installer includes a copy, with its license, so the library can run entirely on your own PC. Thank you to all its contributors.
  • The Unicode Consortium, for encoding every IPA symbol, so they can be typed, shared and searched anywhere.
  • SIL International, for the free Doulos SIL and Charis SIL fonts we recommend for beautiful IPA text (not included here; get them from SIL).
  • GitHub, for hosting the source code and the online version.

With gratitude

This add-in is an independent, personal project. It isn't endorsed by the speakers, the International Phonetic Association, or any of the organizations above. It simply exists because of what they've generously shared. Per-file sources, sizes and SHA-256 checksums for the recordings are kept in src/assets/audio/provenance.json in the project.

We hope passing their work along this way helps speech-language pathologists (SLPs), students and everyone else who transcribes speech. The library is a work in progress. If you have an idea, or spot a mistake (including in these credits), please tell us.
